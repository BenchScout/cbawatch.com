Where this site stands

No both-sidesing here. This is a fan site with a position, and the position is this:

1. Cap it. Finally.

The NFL, NBA, and NHL all live under salary caps. MLB is the last major North American league where a payroll can simply be a function of your owner's ambition and your TV market. That's not a free market — it's a head start, purchased annually.

2. The Dodgers broke it.

Back-to-back champions, a luxury-tax payroll approaching $400 million, and more than $1 billion in deferred salary pushed decades into the future. It's a brilliant front office exploiting a broken system — and it's ruining the integrity of the game.

3. Tax the tankers too.

A cap without a floor just pads owners' pockets. Pair the cap with a competitive integrity tax: teams that pocket revenue-sharing checks while running $70M payrolls should pay for the privilege of not trying. Spend on the roster, or pay the league.

The deferral shell game

Shohei Ohtani's contract pays him $2 million a year now and $68 million a year starting in 2034. Multiply that trick across a roster and the Dodgers have kicked more than a billion dollars of salary into the 2030s and 2040s — buying superteams today with tomorrow's money, while the luxury tax shrugs.

Total Dodgers deferred obligations $0B+ owed through 2046 — some of it to players who will be in their mid-40s when the checks clear

To be clear about the villain here: it isn't Ohtani, and it isn't even really the Dodgers' front office, which is simply playing the rules as written better than anyone else. The villain is a CBA that lets present-day dominance be financed with accounting. When the best-run, richest team can also legally discount its own payroll for tax purposes, the "competitive balance tax" is neither about balance nor much of a tax. A hard cap counts money when the value is delivered — problem solved.

The fix: cap, floor, and a competitive integrity tax

Three mechanisms, one principle — every team pays for the privilege of not competing, whether they're buying pennants or pocketing checks.

1

A hard salary cap

A real ceiling, not a soft "tax line" that the Dodgers treat as a cover charge. Deferred money counts at full present value in the year the player plays. No team buys a five-year head start again.

CAP
2

A salary floor

If there's a ceiling for the Dodgers, there's a floor for the Pirates, Marlins, and A's. Every club spends a guaranteed minimum on major-league payroll — revenue sharing becomes roster investment, not owner profit.

FLOOR
3

The competitive integrity tax

Miss the floor? Pay the difference — plus a penalty — into a pool distributed to teams that do spend. Tanking stops being a business model the moment it has an invoice attached.

TAX DUE

Yes, the union has always treated a cap as a non-starter, and yes, that fight is exactly why the coming lockout could be long and ugly. But a cap with a floor is a different proposal than the one the players rejected in 1994: it guarantees the middle and bottom of the league spends, it widens the number of real bidders for every free agent, and it means a kid in Pittsburgh grows up with the same October odds as a kid in Los Angeles. That's worth missing some spring training games for.

Road to the lockout

  1. Dec 2, 2021

    The last lockout

    Owners lock the players out for 99 days — the sport's first work stoppage in a quarter century. It ends with a bigger tax threshold and no cap. The core dispute is merely postponed.

  2. Dec 9, 2023

    The $680M deferral

    Ohtani signs for 10 years and $700 million — $680 million of it deferred to 2034–2043. The contract that proves the tax system can be gamed at scale.

  3. 2024 – 2025

    Back-to-back

    The Dodgers win consecutive World Series titles while running the largest payroll in the sport's history. Fans of 25+ franchises begin their winters in November knowing how this movie ends.

  4. 2025 – 2026

    Cap talk goes public

    Ownership's appetite for a salary cap moves from whispers to open signaling. The union calls it a non-starter. Both sides quietly prepare for war.

  5. Dec 1, 2026

    The CBA expires

    11:59 PM ET. A lockout is widely expected the moment the clock strikes. This is the leverage point — the one moment the system can actually be rebuilt.

  6. 2027

    The season at stake

    Spring training, maybe more, hangs on the fight. If baseball comes back with a cap, a floor, and a competitive integrity tax — it will have been worth it.
