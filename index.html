The deal lapses at 11:59 pm ET on December 1, 2026, and a lockout is expected the moment it does.15 This site argues that's an opportunity — for a salary cap, a salary floor, and a competitive integrity tax.

Three claims, held without apology.

1

MLB needs a salary cap.

The NFL has capped payrolls since 1994, the NBA since 1984, the NHL since 2005.9,10,11 Baseball is the last major league where payroll is purely a function of an owner's ambition and a market's size — which means October is, more than anywhere else in sports, something you can buy.

2

The Dodgers are ruining the integrity of the game.

Not by cheating — by outspending. Back-to-back titles, a record $169.4 million luxury-tax bill,1 and $1.05 billion in salary deferred into the 2040s.5 A brilliant organization exploiting a broken system, in plain sight.

3

Teams that refuse to spend should pay for it.

A cap without a floor just enriches owners. Pair it with a competitive integrity tax: clubs that pocket revenue-sharing checks while fielding $62 million rosters pay the shortfall — plus a penalty — to the teams that actually compete. Even the owners' own proposal concedes the principle, pairing a $245.3 million cap with a $171.2 million floor.14

The "deterrent" that deters no one.

The competitive balance tax is baseball's only brake on spending — and the teams it targets treat it as a cover charge. In 2024, a record nine teams paid a record $311 million in penalties.4 In 2025 the record fell again: $402.6 million, with the Dodgers alone paying $169.4 million on their way to a second straight title.1

A tax you can simply pay is not a limit — it's a price, and the richest team in the sport keeps deciding it's a bargain. The Dodgers have now paid more in tax over two seasons than most clubs spend on their entire roster in one.1,4

Buying today with tomorrow's money.

Shohei Ohtani's contract pays him $2M a year now and $68M a year from 2034 to 2043.7,8 Repeat that structure across a roster and you can field a superteam whose true cost barely registers against the tax. It's not cheating. That's the problem — it's legal.

The accounting is the scandal. MLB discounts deferred money to present value, so the league officially scores the Dodgers' 2026 payroll at $316.6 million when the roster actually costs $395.2 million2 — and Ohtani's $70 million season counts as roughly $46 million against the tax.7 The villain isn't Ohtani, and it isn't really the Dodgers' front office, which plays the written rules better than anyone. The villain is a CBA that lets present-day dominance be financed with a discount rate. A cap that counts salary at full value in the season it's earned closes the loophole in a sentence.

Three mechanisms. One principle:
pay to not compete.

Hard salary cap

A real ceiling, not a tax line the Dodgers treat as a cover charge. Deferred salary counts at full value in the year it's earned — no present-value discount, no five-year head start.

Salary floor

If there's a ceiling in Los Angeles, there's a floor in Cleveland, Miami, and Sacramento. Even ownership's proposal puts it at $171.2 million14 — nearly triple what the Guardians spent to open 2026.2

Competitive integrity tax

Miss the floor, pay the difference plus a penalty into a pool for the clubs that do spend. Tanking stops being a business model the moment it comes with an invoice.

The union has always called a cap a non-starter — it struck for 232 days in 1994–95 over exactly this,15 and that history is why the coming lockout could be long.17,19 But a cap with a floor is a different deal than the one the players killed in 1994: it forces the bottom half of the league into the market, widens the bidding for every free agent, and gives a kid in Cleveland the same October odds as a kid in Los Angeles. Baseball's own 2022 settlement raised minimum salaries 23% overnight12 — proof the money exists. What's missing is the mechanism. That's worth missing spring training over.

How we got to the brink.

Aug 1994

The strike

Players walk out over a proposed salary cap. The stoppage lasts 232 days and cancels a World Series for the only time in history — the wound both sides still argue about.15

Dec 2021

The lockout

Owners lock the players out for 99 days — the first stoppage in a quarter century. It ends March 10, 2022 with a higher tax threshold, a 23% bump in minimum salary, no cap, and the core dispute postponed.12,13

Dec 2023

The $680M deferral

Ohtani signs for ten years, $700 million — $680 million of it deferred to 2034–2043, discounting his own cap hit by a third. Proof the tax system can be gamed at scale.7,8

2024–25

Back-to-back

The Dodgers win consecutive World Series while setting records for payroll and tax paid — $103 million in 2024, $169.4 million in 2025.1,4 Most of the league starts its winter in November, knowing how the movie ends.

2025–26

Cap talk goes public

Ownership's appetite for a cap moves from whispers to a formal framework — reportedly a $245.3 million ceiling over a $171.2 million floor. The union calls the concept a non-starter. Both sides prepare for a fight.14,17,18

Dec 1, 2026

The CBA expires

11:59 pm ET. A lockout is expected the moment the clock strikes — the one point of leverage where the system can actually be rebuilt.15,19

2027

The season at stake

Spring training, maybe more, rides on the outcome. If baseball returns with a cap, a floor, and an integrity tax — worth it.

Baseball survives lockouts. It won't survive another decade of one team playing a different game than the other twenty-nine.
