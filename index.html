01

The position

NO BOTH-SIDESING

This site has a rooting interest, and it isn't a team — it's the sport. Three articles of the case, entered into the record:

  1. I.

    Cap it. Finally.

    The NFL, NBA, and NHL all live under salary caps. MLB is the last major league where a payroll is simply a function of an owner's ambition and a TV market. That is not a free market — it's a head start, purchased annually.

    [ CEILING ]
  2. II.

    The Dodgers broke it.

    Back-to-back champions. A luxury-tax payroll pushing $400 million. Over a billion dollars of salary deferred into the 2040s. A brilliant front office exploiting a broken system — and ruining the integrity of the game in broad daylight.

    [ EXHIBITS A–B ]
  3. III.

    Tax the tankers too.

    A cap without a floor just pads owners' pockets. Pair it with a competitive integrity tax: clubs that pocket revenue-sharing checks while fielding $70M rosters pay for the privilege of not trying. Spend on the team, or pay the league.

    [ FLOOR + PENALTY ]

03

Exhibit B — the shell game

DEFERRED MONEY, OWED THRU 2046

Shohei Ohtani is paid $2 million a year today and $68 million a year starting in 2034. Run that trick across a roster and you can buy a superteam now with money the luxury tax barely sees. It isn't cheating. That's the problem — it's legal.

TOTAL DODGERS DEFERRED OBLIGATIONS $0B+ owed through 2046 — some of it to players who will be in their mid-forties when the checks clear

To be clear about the villain: it isn't Ohtani, and it isn't really the Dodgers' front office, which is playing the rules as written better than anyone else. The villain is a CBA that lets present-day dominance be financed with accounting. A hard cap that counts deferred money at full present value — in the year the player actually plays — ends the shell game in one sentence.

04

The remedy

CAP · FLOOR · INTEGRITY TAX
  1. R.1

    A hard salary cap

    A real ceiling — not a soft "tax line" the Dodgers treat as a cover charge. Deferrals count at full present value in the year the value is delivered. Nobody buys a five-year head start again.

    ▲
  2. R.2

    A salary floor

    If there's a ceiling for Los Angeles, there's a floor for Pittsburgh, Miami, and Sacramento. Every club spends a guaranteed minimum on major-league payroll — revenue sharing becomes roster investment, not owner profit.

    ▼
  3. R.3

    The competitive integrity tax

    Miss the floor? Pay the difference — plus a penalty — into a pool distributed to clubs that do spend. Tanking stops being a business model the moment it comes with an invoice.

    $

Yes, the union has always called a cap a non-starter, and yes, that fight is exactly why the coming lockout could be long and ugly. But a cap with a floor is a different deal than the one the players struck against in 1994: it forces the bottom half of the league to spend, widens the market for every free agent, and gives a kid in Pittsburgh the same October odds as a kid in Los Angeles. That's worth missing spring training for.

05

Road to the lockout

2021 → 2027
  1. DEC 02
    2021

    The last lockout

    Owners lock the players out for 99 days — the first stoppage in a quarter century. It ends with a higher tax threshold and no cap. The core dispute is merely postponed.

  2. DEC 09
    2023

    The $680M deferral

    Ohtani signs for ten years and $700 million — $680 million of it deferred to 2034–2043. The contract that proves the tax system can be gamed at scale.

  3. '24–'25

    Back-to-back

    The Dodgers win consecutive World Series titles running the largest payroll in the sport's history. Fans of twenty-five-plus franchises start their winters in November, knowing how the movie ends.

  4. '25–'26

    Cap talk goes public

    Ownership's appetite for a cap moves from whispers to open signaling. The union calls it a non-starter. Both sides quietly prepare for war.

  5. DEC 01
    2026

    The CBA expires

    11:59 pm ET. A lockout is widely expected the moment the clock strikes. This is the leverage point — the one moment the system can actually be rebuilt.

  6. 2027

    The season at stake

    Spring training — maybe more — hangs on the fight. If baseball comes back with a cap, a floor, and an integrity tax, it will have been worth every missed game.
